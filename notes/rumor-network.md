# The Rumor Network — Morrow sightings and their circulation

Working map (2026-07-12) of every account of "the man who follows the
white buffalo": who saw what, when, who told whom, and which phrases
belong to whom. Undergirds the four depositions Farrell hears (05
drunk, 07 trapper, 07 Creighton, 09 trader). Rumors are loopy — the
network is a DAG only until tellers re-hear their own stories coming
back up the road; provenance is deliberately unrecoverable past one
hop, and the text says so exactly once.

## Ground truth (firsthand sightings; dates/places per geo-ledger)

- **Trader** — ~Aug 1858, at the post (Middle Loup forks). Earliest
  and closest: Morrow passed northbound weeks after the murders.
  ISOLATED — nearest white man two days off; no circulating version
  existed yet for him to borrow. His account forms alone.
- **McCulloch** (trapper, never on stage) — ~early Nov 1858,
  sandhills/Niobrara country. Tells it in Council Bluffs ~mid-Nov
  ("came through last month," 05), then leaves the book.
- **Fort Kearny trapper** — ~Nov 18, on the Niobrara ("maybe three
  weeks back" from ~Dec 9–11, 07).
- **Creighton** — ~Nov 30, crossing the upper North Loup (the herd's
  south loop; they were at or near the turn, so his sighting has no
  reliable heading — see Flattening, below).
- **Rumor cloud** — unnamed others; by December "everyone would have
  a cousin who had seen the man" (07:133).

## Transmission edges

- **McCulloch → Council Bluffs saloons (~Nov):** coins THE TAGS —
  "thin as a rail," "beard down to his chest," half-dead, the eyes
  that warned him off. The drunk (05, ~Dec 9) repeats them nearly
  verbatim and is honest about the sourcing ("I'm just telling you
  what McCulloch said"). His telling is McCulloch's, not his; its
  fidelity is the point. DO NOT differentiate it.
- **The corridor (Council Bluffs ↔ trail ↔ Fort Kearny):** the tags
  circulate all November–December. Creighton, the mail carrier, is
  the corridor's principal VECTOR — he carries everything else that
  moves along the road, and he plausibly spread McCulloch's telling
  for weeks BEFORE his own sighting.
- **Creighton (07):** a firsthand sighting narrated in secondhand
  clothes — he opens with the circulating tags ("thin as a rail,
  beard down past his chest") and breaks through to his own coin
  only at the eyes ("like looking at a dead man who hadn't had the
  sense to fall down yet"). Farrell clocks the recycled phrasing on
  the page and rules provenance unrecoverable. (The borrowed-language
  registry's grep discipline, diegeticized.)
- **Fort Kearny trapper (07):** the RESISTOR. Animal-fixated,
  professionally distrustful of other men's words; refuses the
  man-tags outright — "The man I can't tell you much about. Thin,
  and a steady walker." His coinages are all his own (white to the
  ground in November; no hunter walks behind his prey; the hide
  priced against whose country she walks in).
- **Trader (09):** the UPSTREAM ISOLATE. Shares nothing with the
  corridor: "beard like a bird's nest," "like I was a tree or a
  rock," "given up... on all of it."

## Doctrine

- **Sameness is a transmission fingerprint, not a lapse.** The two
  depositions sharing verbatim tags (drunk, Creighton) are the two
  connected by the mail road. The two isolated witnesses (trapper by
  temperament, trader by geography) coin independently.
- **Convergence-without-contact is the second signal.** All four
  accounts register the eyes — including the trader, who cannot have
  heard anyone else's version. The standard description could be
  contamination or could be accuracy; both are true; Farrell cannot
  separate them and neither can the reader. The text names this once
  (Farrell's beat in 07 at Creighton's telling) and never again.
- **Farrell's own flattening:** 15's "north when the trader saw him,
  north when the mail carrier passed him, north when the trapper
  watched him" attributes a heading only the trader actually stated
  (09:89 "on his way north"). Deliberate — Farrell compresses story
  into direction when he needs one; Ch 7's close corrects him
  ("the tracks were the only thing... I could put my hand on.
  North was what I had," 13:109).
