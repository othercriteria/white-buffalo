# Henry Farrell — working notes

Compiled 2026-07-12 from the week's instrument findings and rulings.
Citations are drafts NN:LL.

## 1. The violence design (item CLOSED 2026-07-12, DK)

Farrell's dangerousness is asserted only through laundered channels,
never authorially: reputation ("something dangerous, something
barely contained," 01:69 — undercut in the same paragraph: "looked
merely tired"); his own deadpan repetition of hearsay ("they say I'm
not particular about what happens when I find them," 01:107 —
neither confirmed nor performed); physical residue (scarred
knuckles, 01:89; the war rifle that "had not failed me yet," 03:43);
flat intention ("I would kill him," 03:77, with precedent implied by
"These things rarely were"). The CAPACITY is shown, displaced into
memory — the Chapultepec dream (17:55-67) is the book's most violent
passage and it is his: "my hands doing what my hands had learned to
do." Present tense carries only readiness-texture (hand near the
scabbard, 11:65; hand on the rifle in the night, 13:105).

THE DESIGN: the whole book is the brink. Two months riding toward a
paid killing, discharged at "I did not raise the rifle" (20:29).
Showing violence en route would spend the ending, add the first-half
incident every reader warned against, and blur the conduct-difference
that carries the moral architecture (Brandt warned off, the horse
talked off the ice, the buffalo spared). A regression reader flagged
the never-shown violence and ruled it "a want, not a need" in the
same breath, preferring the Brandt scene to any gunfight. CLOSED as
designed; future instrument flags are landing-data.

## 2. Registers he owns (register pass, 2026-07-11)

- Money and prices on everything (fifty cents and no questions,
  03:5; "I paid that too," 09:115; the hundred dollars in the final
  inventory, 20:73). Morrow's economy is sacrificial; Farrell's is
  commercial. Neither narrator can borrow the other's.
- Estimates in "maybe"/"about"/flat numbers/"I judged" — never
  "perhaps" (sole exception: the protected fifty-yards rhyme,
  20:21). Morrow keeps "perhaps" exclusively.
- Retrospective hindsight-irony, structurally barred to Morrow
  ("Fort Kearny was three days ahead. I made it in four," 07:41).
- Forensic self-audit with costs attached ("I made it fit, because
  the tracks said what they said," 13:73; "the way I should have
  done at the creek weeks before," 17:45).
- Deflationary irony ("It was not complicated. These things rarely
  were," 03:79 — template owned by Farrell alone; Morrow's twin was
  cut).
- Dialogue: he lives among named, priced, talking people (quote
  marks 30-88 per chapter; zero in every Morrow file).

## 3. The flattening reader (#42, RATIFIED 2026-07-11)

His one act of sympathetic reading garbles its object: "not sorry
for any of it" (19:57) erases Morrow's precise line between costs
and project ("not sorry for THIS," 18:73-75), manufacturing the
"both things both times" paradox he reports. Rhymes with "I made it
fit" — reading-as-violence at the sentence level. Intentional;
arose in drafting, adopted because characteristic.

## 4. Distinctions from Morrow that must survive revision

- His hollowness has a chemical calendar (the counted swallows,
  11:19-27) where Morrow's is metaphysical; his ghosts arrive on
  the withdrawal schedule (the book's certified original stroke).
- He does small, costly, life-tending things under no obligation:
  the Brandt advice, the horse off the ice, cottonwood bark, the
  unfinished draft returned "on top of the paper" (11:97-99).
- He renders judgment where Morrow renders theology: "choices I
  could not forgive" (19:61) — the dissenting voice #29 keeps alive.
- The naming-refusal, the "Whether X or Y" hedge, and the sacral
  lexicon are Morrow's; if they appear in Farrell pre-reading, it is
  bleed (registry rules apply).
