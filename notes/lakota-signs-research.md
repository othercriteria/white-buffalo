# Lakota presence-sign research (2026-07-11, for stack #41 repair 3)

Web-verified against digitized primary sources; full citations in the
session transcript. What is ATTESTED vs what would be invention.

## Attested signs (observable from outside, no ventriloquism)
- **Boller 1858-62 (exact years): buffalo skulls on a lookout butte
  summit, scarlet cloth bound around each horn, beside peeled poles
  flying calico — explicitly offerings "that he would send them
  plenty of buffalo."** The strongest usable sign; pairs with the
  book's existing stake (08:63).
- Boller: vermilion-painted cairn + skull + arrow bundle as landmark;
  later found SCATTERED — a sign that changes state, readable news.
- Denig c.1854: valuables carried alone into the hills, deliberately
  RUINED, and left — the damage is the point; misreadable as trash
  by a greenhorn.
- Kurz 1851: a tanned WHITE CALFSKIN hung on a hilltop as a sun
  offering — stolen in the dark by a white man. The premise in
  miniature. (Available as deep allusion; unused for now.)
- Parkman 1846 / Kurz 1851: scaffold burials at distance, buffalo
  skulls "arranged in the mystic circle," red blanket wrapping,
  medicine pouch tied to a post.
- **Warren 1857: Hunkpapa/Miniconjou bands physically halted his
  survey to protect buffalo herds they were holding; he waited three
  days.** Documentary warrant for active Lakota policing of white
  access to buffalo, our years, our region.

## Tracking vocabulary (Marcy, The Prairie Traveler, 1859)
- Lodge-pole drag marks = families moving (no war party transports
  lodges); NO pole marks = war or hunting party.
- Unshod, smaller pony tracks vs American shod horses.
- Dung scattered along the trail = ridden horses kept moving; piled
  = loose/wild horses.
- Trail aging: sand crusted on pressed grass spears (dew-dated);
  sharp vs rain-softened edges; grass bent showing "a different
  shade of green" sighted along the direction of travel.

## White buffalo: attested vs invention
- Attested: the TAKEN ROBE complex — consecration, pole-hoisting as
  sun offering (Catlin, Mandan 1832); fifteen horses' price
  (Maximilian 1833-34); two racehorses for a white robe, open
  market, 1851 (Kurz); Fletcher 1884 (Hunkpapa, near-period): the
  living white buffalo "remains near the centre of the herd...
  considered as the chief or sacred one of the herd"; killing one =
  a FEARED OBLIGATION (expensive rites); news travels beyond the
  tribe, purchase offers from remote points, promises exacted
  beforehand. NOTE: the book's herd-deference canon (04:11) matches
  Fletcher unprompted.
- NOT attested: any ceremony around a LIVING white animal.
  Guardianship must be built behavioral (watchers, signs, pickets)
  from Warren 1857 + Fletcher — never ceremonial. DO NOT INVENT.
- "Denig on white robe prices" is UNVERIFIED — cite Maximilian/
  Catlin/Kurz/Fletcher instead.

## Commercial threat, late 1850s
Ordinary robe ≈ $3 country value (Denig's Fort Union ledger); robe
trade ~150k/year at Fort Union's 1850s peak; a white robe = 15
horses (Maximilian) or two racehorses (Kurz) ≈ 20-150x ordinary.
Later cases: $200 (Miles City 1882), $1,000 cash (Dodge 1873). The
demand side, the price gradient, and white theft of white-hide
offerings (Kurz) are all attested; a known living white buffalo
drawing white hunters is modest extrapolation.
