# Training set manifest: travois STRUCTURE supplement

Acquired 2026-07-09 to correct the model's travois physicality (pole crossing
and lashing at the withers/saddle, load rack/netting placement between poles,
butt-end drag angle). Complements manifest.md; files here are new additions
only — nothing in the original set was touched.

Sources: Wissler 1910 (AMNH Anthropological Papers Vol. 5 Pt. 1, archive.org
item `materialcultureo00wiss`) and Ewers 1955, BAE Bulletin 159 (archive.org
item `dli.ministry.14275`, unrestricted Digital Library of India scan of the
US Government publication), plus two PD photographs via Wikimedia Commons.

Page images pulled with the `https://archive.org/download/<id>/page/n<N>.jpg`
pattern. All files verified as real JPEG/TIFF data with `file`.

## Line diagrams (diagram- prefix; native monochrome register)

### diagram-wissler-fig56-travois-types.jpg
- Title: Fig. 56 a (50-5724), b (6158), "Types of Travois" — *Material Culture of the Blackfoot Indians*, Clark Wissler, p. 89
- Source: https://archive.org/download/materialcultureo00wiss/page/n94.jpg
- Date: published 1910 (Anthropological Papers of the AMNH, Vol. 5 Pt. 1)
- Resolution: 2048x3437 (full-page figure)
- Medium: line drawing (book figure, full page)
- PD rationale: published 1910 (pre-1930); Wissler d. 1947, US publication
- Structure taught: overhead/plan view of both travois frame types — (a) netted oval hoop lashed between the poles, (b) ladder-like transverse-bar rack — plus the apex lashing wrap where the two poles cross and the loose hitch thongs. Facing text (leaf n95, printed p. 90, not downloaded) describes the lashing run in detail.

### diagram-ewers-fig18-horse-travois-construction.jpg
- Title: Figure 18, "Construction of a Blackfoot horse travois" — *The Horse in Blackfoot Indian Culture*, John C. Ewers, BAE Bulletin 159, p. 104
- Source: https://archive.org/download/dli.ministry.14275/page/n122.jpg
- Date: published 1955
- Resolution: 3253x5070 (figure printed sideways on page — ROTATE 90° before use)
- Medium: labeled line diagram (book figure)
- PD rationale: US Government publication (Smithsonian Bureau of American Ethnology), no copyright; scan from unrestricted archive.org item
- Structure taught: THE key image. Side view of travois rigged on a standing horse with every element lettered: shafts (a, a'), forward tie (b), rawhide hitch pad at the withers (c), rawhide hitch and pole-wrapping line (d), girth strap (e), rawhide cord saddle ties (f, f'), primary and secondary struts of the loading platform (g, h). Shows exactly how the poles cross and lash over the withers and how the rack sits between the poles.

### diagram-ewers-fig19-lodgepole-hitch.jpg
- Title: Figure 19, "The Blackfoot lodgepole hitch" — Ewers, BAE Bulletin 159, p. 107
- Source: https://archive.org/download/dli.ministry.14275/page/n125.jpg
- Date: published 1955
- Resolution: 3112x5070 (figure embedded in text page; crop)
- Medium: line diagram (book figure)
- PD rationale: US Government publication, no copyright
- Structure taught: close rear-quarter view of bundled lodgepoles lashed over a wood saddle — the pole-over-saddle crossing geometry from a second angle, with the rawhide line threaded through burned holes in the pole ends and tied over the animal's back. Teaches the withers crossing even when no travois rack is present.

### diagram-ewers-fig26-travois-load-backrest.jpg
- Title: Figure 26 — a, "Placement of a willow backrest on the bottom of a travois load"; b, method of transporting water in a paunch container — Ewers, BAE Bulletin 159, p. 135

- Date: published 1955
- Resolution: 3084x5095 (figure embedded in text page; crop)
- Medium: line diagram (book figure)
- PD rationale: US Government publication, no copyright
- Structure taught: rear/oblique view of a loaded travois behind the horse — how the load platform sits between the diverging poles behind the hips, backrest laid flat on the rack, paunch bag slung from a pole; the drag section geometry from the load back to the butt ends.

- Title: Plate 5 — a, "Piegan lodges, showing methods of storing horse travois in camp" (photo taken before 1890, courtesy Montana Historical Society); b, "Travois used as a litter, Crow Indians" (photo taken before 1900) — Ewers, BAE Bulletin 159, plates following p. 358
- Source: https://archive.org/download/dli.ministry.14275/page/n380.jpg
- Date: photos pre-1890 / pre-1900; published 1955
- Resolution: 3160x5105 (two panels on one plate page; crop)
- Medium: halftone reproductions of 19th-century photographs
- PD rationale: US Government publication, no copyright; underlying photos pre-1900
- Structure taught: b is the payoff — side profile of a saddled horse with travois rigged as a litter, poles crossed and lashed over the saddle, a person lying on the rack, butt ends on the ground at a shallow drag angle. a shows travois parked upright against lodges (how they read when not in draft).

## Not acquired (and why)
- Wissler Plate VIII ("A Travois and ..."): the plate leaves at the end of the
  `materialcultureo00wiss` scan are blank — plates missing from that copy.
  The figure text (p. 90) still references it. Could be retried from the
  Google-scanned copy `materialculture00wissgoog` or the full AMNH volume.
- Walter McClintock travois photographs: his Commons material is tipi-focused
  (no travois subjects found); Yale's digital collection is JS-rendered and
  not reachable through the fetch tools used here. The Beinecke Flickr album
  remains an untapped source if more photographic rigging references are wanted.
- Otis Mason, "Primitive Travel and Transportation" (1896): full-text search
  of `primitivetravelt00masoiala` shows travois treated only in passing (litter
  context, fig. 234); no dedicated travois construction figure found.
- LOC "Travois - Piegan" (2006675462) and "A Blackfoot travois" (2004673269):
  rights "not evaluated," no dates on the caption cards — PD case too weak.
- Ewers Figure 8 (breaking a horse to the travois): shows a hide-drag training
  scene, not travois structure. Inspected and skipped.
