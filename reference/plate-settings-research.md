# Plate-settings research: Fort Kearny, trading post, moving village
(2026-07-09; full agent report in session transcript)

## Fort Kearny, Dec 1858 — text CONFIRMED, one absence flagged
- Unstockaded ✓ (Mills photo, June 1858 — the ground-truth image; no
  wall until 1864-65). Defense was artillery among the trees.
- Mix confirmed: ~5 unpainted frame buildings (incl. TWO-STORY CO
  quarters and 70x30 barracks) + ~two dozen long low sod/adobe
  buildings spilling outward; 4-acre parade ground, central flagstaff.
- FLAGGED ABSENCE: the planted cottonwoods (1848; ~15-25 ft by 1858;
  "the only trees visible in any direction") are famous and not in the
  text. Enrichment candidate, DK to rule.
- Approach from the east: flagstaff and bare tree-line first, then low
  brown sod roofs + a few two-story frame blocks; an open village,
  chimneys smoking.

## Small trading post (Bordeaux analog) — one soft spot
- 20x15 room, plank counter, candles, lean-to stable: all CONFIRMED.
- Sagging log-and-turf roof: confirmed and characteristic.
- SOFT SPOT: sod WALLS are Platte-valley idiom; Niobrara/White River
  posts were log (or log banked with sod) — timber available. Text
  tweak candidate: "low house of cottonwood logs banked with sod" —
  one clause, DK to rule. Cast-iron stove: possible (freighted), keep;
  exterior tell is a guyed stovepipe, smoke-blackened snow.
- Winter texture: hides and firewood stacked against walls.

## Brulé village moving, autumn, from two miles — text survives via terrain
- Broad front (quarter-to-half-mile) is the norm; single file is wrong
  — but the text's "strung out along a ridgeline" (12:59) is saved by
  terrain constraint, and "line bent wider... river around a snag" is
  broad-front behavior. No tweak needed.
- Silhouette signature: travois pole-bundles crossing above the withers,
  butts dragging at 30-40° — long low triangles on dozens of horses;
  load platforms; dog travois at ankle height; the LOOSE HERD (5-15
  head/lodge — hundreds of animals) as a separate faster-shifting mass;
  outrider specks. Walking pace (~3.5 km/h); thin leg-height dust haze
  at most in dry autumn.
- Imagery: Catlin "Band of Sioux Moving Camp" (SAAM); Miller watercolors
  (Walters). Bodmer weak for the moving column.
