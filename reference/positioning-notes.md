# Positioning notes (harvested from the review program, 2026-07-11)

## The original strokes (per the harshest instrument, the comparatist)
1. "Monomania as patient, logistical suicide — waiting for your death
   to grow up — is a premise none of the ancestors owns."
2. "A pursuit narrative in which pursuer and pursued never meet...
   every contact is posthumous and textual... the novella's true
   climax is a man reading. A Western composed almost entirely of
   aftermath... that structure is not in Melville, Johnson, O'Brien,
   McCarthy, Ballard, or the television. It is the one place the book
   stops inheriting and builds."
3. (hostile comparatist v2, 2026-07-11, in a review whose verdict was
   otherwise "Pass"): "None of the eight models makes delirium
   tremens the engine of a wilderness pursuit... Timing the hunter's
   haunting to withdrawal while the hunted's haunting stays
   metaphysical gives the two narrations a genuinely original hinge —
   the ghosts arrive on a chemical schedule and are never explained
   away. That device is the book's own invention."
Pitch leads from these, NOT from the genre furniture.

## Nearest-shelf comps (converged across six readers)
Butcher's Crossing (closest by a wide margin), The Crossing (the wolf
sections), Train Dreams, "a prairie Moby-Dick at 31,000 words," The
North Water readers, Ron Hansen's Assassination of Jesse James (the
journal's formality). NOT for Portis/Lonesome Dove warmth seekers;
body count four, three deaths offstage.

## Hazard map for jacket copy
- The Sheridan trap: laconic-hard-men marketing invites "prestige-
  frontier product" dismissal ("period detail worn as production
  design... consolation machinery"). Do not sell it as that.
- The McCarthy invitation: comparisons will be made; the book
  survives them on material culture and structure, not on violence —
  never promise violence.
- The AI front-matter: hostile-2 led its review with the disclosure
  ("explains more about the book than its authors may want").
  Positioning conversation needed before submission: the disclosure's
  placement/wording is load-bearing for how critics enter the book.

## The one-line defenses (earned in testing)
- Elided murder: every cold reader scored it earned; the two lines
  Farrell quotes are "more devastating than the scene could be."
- Missed climax: the two hostile instruments contradict each other
  about it — flinch vs "the one place the book builds."
- The Lakota silence: "structurally, the ending says: this was their
  story all along and the narrators were trespassers in it" — taught
  as a limitation with the pairing shelf (reference/classroom-
  apparatus.md).
