# White Buffalo

**Ben Cohen and Daniel Klein**

---

*A novella*

---

The white buffalo is sacred to the Lakota people. The tradition of Ptesáŋwiŋ, the White Buffalo Calf Woman, who brought the sacred pipe and its rites, is a living religious tradition, not a store of material for fiction — and this is a book about a man who takes what is not his. It was written under limits meant to keep it from doing what its subject does: no Lakota person in these pages speaks, no ceremony is invented, and what the narrators see of Lakota life — the stakes, the bound skulls, the bands moving with the herds — is drawn from the period record of white observers, because that record is the only knowledge such men could have had. The sources, and the limits, are part of the book's working record. We do not own the white buffalo, or the lands through which we have followed her, and we do not pretend that the men who tell this story were ever the only people in it.
