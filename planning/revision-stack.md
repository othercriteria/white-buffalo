# Revision stack

Accumulated issues + proposals, second half of the revision project.
Sources: three blind readers (truncated at Ch 5, Ch 7, full), tic
auditor, period auditor (all fresh-context, 2026-07-10), plus my own
full read. NOTHING here is applied yet unless marked. Ordered by my
priority; DK-DECISION items reverse or bend prior rulings and need a
sign-off.

## Confirmed by multiple independent readers

### 1. Frame-year ambiguity (comprehension hazard, cheap fix)
Both truncated readers reverse-engineered Farrell's strand to the
WRONG year (1856; 1857–58 — actual: Nov 1858–Jan 1859) because the
interleave implies concurrency with the facing journal dates and the
frame is never dated. Full reader: "fair play, but only just." All the
internal clues agree on 1858 but none is explicit.
**Proposal:** date Catherine's last letter once, in Ch 2 — "The last
letter was dated May the twentieth, eighteen fifty-eight." (03:13).
One phrase; kills the hazard; the staggered-timeline double-click that
reader C praised still works — it just becomes checkable.

### 2. Civil War prophecy paragraph (all three readers, worst offender)
05:53–57: Davis/Lee named and résuméd, "would be generals when the
time came," "the whole country would burn." Reader A: "the one passage
where I felt a hand on my shoulder." C: "phrased for the 2026 reader."
The brothers' Kansas talk and the ferryman's "She'll burn again, God
willing" are unanimously praised — the damage is only the editorial.
Note Ch 2's unnamed-senator beat (03:55) is the same move at lower
volume; A counted three coy-famous-man moves in ten pages.
**Proposal:** keep the brothers and ferryman untouched. Cut or ground
the 05:53–57 editorial to what a veteran can know without prophecy:
he has SEEN these men do arithmetic with lives; let him stop at the
memory and the unease, no futures named. Keep the Ch 2 newspaper beat
(it's personal — he watched the man lead volunteers at Monterrey) but
that's the only one that survives at full strength.

### 3. Protected borrowings: counter-brief received — DK-DECISION
Reader C (cold, full text) hit exactly the device (b)+(c) DK approved:
"'A great and quiet peace' ... and 'like a ship through water' ...
Two independent narrators coining identical phrases reads as the
author's hand on the page. If convergence is the intent, it needs a
signal; as is, it's a tic, and it lands at the worst possible moment."
The bleed is real (all three borrows are post-journal-reading, Ch 11
only) but the causality is not legible to a strong cold reader.
**Options:**
(a) Hold the line — some rereaders will catch that the echoes begin
    only after Farrell reads the journal; the recursive-read beat in
    Ch 10 is the intended key.
(b) Plant the source: in Ch 10, among the fragments Farrell reads
    aloud/quotes from the journal, include the ship-through-water
    sentence (he already quotes two passages verbatim). Then Ch 11's
    reuse reads as haunting, not tic. One sentence, non-winking.
(c) Reduce to one borrow (weakens the device to invisibility).
**My lean: (b)** — it keeps DK's device and answers the defect with
the book's own grammar.
**Auditor addendum:** there is a FOURTH sanctioned echo (the waiting
closer, 16:103 → 19:75) that continuity.md's "stay at three" never
counted — ledgers now reconciled in borrowed-language.md. Cutting
19:75 (item 11) plus fix (b) here leaves exactly three borrows, all
in Ch 11, source planted. That is my combined recommendation.

### 4. JE VI treads water (weakest unit per B and C)
Third/fourth "she is beautiful / she is sacred" declarations recur
from JE III and V "at diminishing returns" — the Nov 19 subsection
(12:85–99) restates both at length. The unit's real cargo (village
passing, "the only calendar I keep now," date-doubt, the hollow) is
strong and stays.
**Proposal:** compress 12:85–99 — keep "She is the most beautiful
thing I have ever seen" and the certainty-of-death turn, cut the
comparative catalog (Virginia autumns, Gulf sunsets, candlelight) or
reduce to one item. ~80 words out; the unit stops rhyming with itself.

### 5. Rumor-chain distribution (A and C agree; count is right)
Four tellings escalate correctly, but the trapper and Creighton share
Ch 4 and describe the same three details in nearly the same words
(thin, beard, steady walk, empty eyes). A: "maintenance." C: "a
trapper and a mail rider should not see identically."
**Proposal:** differentiate what each man noticed. The trapper (30
years on the plains) gets the ANIMAL: biggest cow he ever saw, wrong
color, wrong season, the herd hanging back from her. Creighton keeps
the MAN and the eyes. Trim the trapper's man-description to a clause.
DK's "dilution" instinct lands here as subtraction, not addition —
all three readers warn against adding buffalo material to Farrell's
first half.

### 6. The homestead tracks (C's "genuine mechanical false note")
August tracks cannot read in late-December snowpack (13:57–69), and
the book supplies no re-pass. Also feeds reader B's lone-pair-vs-herd
objection (journal has her always inside two hundred animals; homestead
sign and rumors show a lone pair).
**Proposal (keeps the tracks-north plate intact):** make the sign a
LATE-AUTUMN re-pass, not August residue. The witness chain already
proves the drift: Creighton saw the pair crossing the Loup — south —
in late November. One or two sentences in Ch 7: the tracks are weeks
old, made since the snows began — the herd had come back through the
valley in early winter, and one great animal and one man had walked
the homestead clearing. Morrow wouldn't journal it (JE VIII is three
entries in four months, and the homestead is the thing he does not
write about — 16:5 already establishes his elision). Farrell's "I made
it fit" survives untouched: presence still isn't proof of the killing.
The herd-vs-pair ambiguity elsewhere stays as legend-compression +
withdrawal fog — B found it productive, C never flagged it.

### 7. Theme stated three times in Farrell's voice (C)
Ch 7 ("I had been one of them, for a time"), Ch 9 ("I recognized him
... I did not like how small it was" — C: the best), Ch 10 ("He had
found something to die for. And I had not").
**Proposal:** the two briefs split (C keeps Ch 9's likeness, the
auditor keeps Ch 10's asymmetry). They do different work and both are
short: cut Ch 7's paragraph tail (13:89–93 last two sentences — the
counterfactual is already in the scene), keep Ch 9 ("I did not like
how small it was") and Ch 10 ("He had found something to die for.
And I had not."). Net: three statements become two, likeness once,
difference once.

## Enrichment candidates (DK's menu, reader-motivated)

### 8. JE VII: dramatize the wrestling (C: "asserted, never shown")
"I have wrestled with it for weeks. I have tried to find another way.
I have failed" (14:65) — and a reader can ask why he didn't simply
haze the herd elsewhere. One new dated entry (~120 words) between
July 9 and July 17: he TRIES the other way — walks out at night to
turn the herd off the valley, and she will not be driven; the herd
moves for nothing but her; he stops because pushing risks a stampede
toward the homestead, or her injury. Active wrestling, closes the
logic hole, and the failure is HERS to inflict — which is right.

### 9. One non-buffalo human beat for Farrell's first half (C)
"Brandt is the only living person Farrell affects in the whole first
half; everyone else is a vendor or a rumor-source." One half-page
encounter, no buffalo content, something Farrell does or fails to do
for someone. Natural slot: the generic middle of Ch 3 (the Iowa
tavern C called drag) — replace texture with incident. More than one
would pad; all three readers say the incident/rumor balance otherwise
holds.

### 10. Letters-in-batches softener (C, minor)
"Every month, sometimes more often" (01:93) sits oddly against a
homestead five days past a trading post visited "a few times."
**Proposal:** one clause in Ch 1 or Ch 2 — the letters arrived in
bundles of two and three, as the post allowed. Also quietly covers
the silent ten weeks (see timeline-geography.md).
