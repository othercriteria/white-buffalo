**MEMO — Plate leaves vs. in-text cuts in *White Buffalo***
From: production consulting
Re: whether the eighteen plates should move to dedicated full-page plate leaves

**1. The current scheme**

What you have built is the *wood-engraved trade book* convention, and you have built it correctly. All eighteen cuts are set into the letterpress at content-addressed anchors (`assemble.py` PLACEMENTS, injected via `\bookplate` — centered on the measure with codified air, pinned inline so nothing drifts from its paragraph), on ordinary text pages, counted in pagination, folios printed, uncaptioned, with a List of Illustrations up front keyed to folios. That is precisely what relief-printed illustration did in the 1850s–1880s: wood engravings were type-high and locked into the forme with the text, so they printed *with* the text — in-text vignettes and full-page cuts alike, on text stock, versos printed. The distinct convention your question gestures at — the *plate leaf* (image alone, blank verso, often excluded from pagination, "to face p. 84" in the list) — belongs historically to intaglio and planographic processes (steel engraving, lithography), which could not print in the same pass as type and so went onto separate, often different, paper and were tipped or bound in. Period books commonly mixed both: steel frontispiece plus wood cuts in the text. Your colophon says the plates are drawn "in the idiom of the nineteenth-century wood engraving"; the current inline scheme is the bibliographically honest carrier for that idiom. One refinement of the record: your own format doctrine (planning/image-gen.md, DK 2026-07-09) already declares journal-side portraits to be "full-page plates" and Farrell-side landscapes to be "in-text vignettes" — but the implementation renders the portraits as 85%-width inline cuts that land alone on a page only by accident of breaking. In the current build, offering-stake (phys. p.59) happens to fall alone; first-sighting (p.19), morrow-hollow (p.123), journal-found (p.130), and finale-fifty-yards (p.136) share their pages with two-to-nine-line slivers of text. The doctrine is half-implemented.

**2. The case for dedicated plate leaves**

*Aesthetic.* A full-page plate is a formal event: the text stops, the image holds the whole opening, the reader's pace is arrested. For a book about witnessing, that is not decoration — it enacts the thing. The five portrait cuts are the natural candidates, and they are already doctrinally marked: **first-sighting** (02), **offering-stake** (08), **morrow-hollow** (18), **journal-found** (19), **finale-fifty-yards** (20). Note the emergent structure your own illustration critic ordered protected: journal plates portrait until Chapter Ten's inheritance, after which Farrell's chapters go portrait — journal-found and finale-fifty-yards are the inheritance made visible. Promoting exactly these five to full leaves would make the doctrine legible on the page: portrait leaf = the journal's voice; landscape cut-in-text = Farrell's forensic eye. The strongest single beneficiaries are **morrow-hollow** — it currently shares p.123 with "This is the end of my account… I will not write again," and a full leaf there would close the journal with the finality the text claims — and **finale-fifty-yards**, the book's last image, which deserves to not share its page with anything. *Practical.* Full-page treatment ends the awkward stranded slivers (nine lines above a five-inch cut reads as an accident, not a design), and under `\raggedbottom` it removes the short-page lottery that currently decides which portraits get a page to themselves. *Page-count consequences are mild if versos stay live.* Wood-engraving precedent does not require blank versos (that is the steel-plate tell); full-page cuts printed with text backing them. Five promotions, versos live, no recto-forcing, costs roughly +3 to +5 pages on 140 — since three of the five already consume most of a page. Folio treatment: your gatefold already established the house rule — *counted but unprinted* (`\thispagestyle{empty}`) — and `plate_folios()` measures physical pages, so the List of Illustrations survives unchanged. Recto-forcing I advise against: you already ruled (openany, kept deliberately) that scattered blank versos are wrong for a plated novella, and period full-page wood engravings fell recto and verso alike.

**3. The case against**

*The anchor system is the book's best production idea, and leaves weaken it.* A plate on its own leaf cannot sit "at or just after its anchor paragraph" in the strict sense; it sits at the nearest page boundary. The adjacency guarantee degrades from same-paragraph to same-opening-or-next — the traditional "to face p. N" tolerance. Your assembly map's corollary (a plate must *exceed* its sentence: said-then-sought, said-then-verified) is served by intimacy; the evidence-class landscapes (**graves**, **homestead**, **homestead-interior**, **trading-post**) work precisely because they sit *inside* Farrell's narration as things his eye passes across mid-paragraph. Pulling those onto leaves would aestheticize evidence — frame what should be encountered. *Rhythm.* Eighteen plates in 140 pages is one every 7.7 pages. Full conversion, especially with blank versos, turns a novella with cuts into a picture book with captions omitted: +25–30 pages of interruption, and the two-narrator interleave — whose alternation is the book's pulse — acquires full-stop rests it never asked for. *Geometry.* The thirteen landscape cuts (1216×832, AR 1.46) are physically wrong for a 4.0×~6.8in text block: full measure gives a 2.74in-tall image swimming in white, or you upscale past sense. They are vignettes by aspect, not just by doctrine. *Resolution — the hard gate.* The portraits are 832×1216. At the current 85% (3.4in) they print at 245 ppi, already logged as below your 300 floor. Grown to full measure (4.0in) they drop to ~208 ppi; height-filling is worse. Promotion to leaves *requires* re-rendering the five portraits at 2× (the homestead-alive precedent: 2432×1664, 608 ppi in the current build) or a defensible upscale pass. *Cost.* At POD the page count is trivial money; but note the plate-leaf signal barely survives POD anyway — same stock both sides, no tip-in, no plate sheen. Only the layout signal survives. (The gatefold, separately, has already committed you past standard POD: no printer binds a 10.75in leaf into a 5.5in trim. That leaf is hand-bindery or digital-artifact territory regardless of this decision.)

**4. Hybrid options**

(a) **The doctrinal hybrid — promote the five portraits to full leaves, keep the thirteen landscapes inline.** This is the format doctrine's own text, finally implemented; it converts an accidental distinction (which portraits happen to fall alone) into a deliberate one. The two ruled journal-side landscape flexes (speaking-to-her, homestead-alive) stay inline — the flex rulings hold. (b) **Minimal hybrid — promote only morrow-hollow and finale-fifty-yards**, the two endings, leaving the earlier portraits as cuts; defensible but it makes the portrait grammar inconsistent and forfeits the inheritance legibility. (c) **Full conversion** — rejected above; it imports steel-plate bibliography into a wood-engraving book. On captions: keep none. Period full-page cuts usually carried engraved titles, but your caption doctrine is settled ("boundary grammar is whitespace, not furniture"), the List of Illustrations already names every plate, and an uncaptioned full leaf reads as confident, not incomplete. Do not introduce captions as a rider on this change.

**5. Recommendation**

**Adopt hybrid (a): the five portrait plates move to dedicated full-page leaves, versos live, folios counted but unprinted, no recto-forcing, no captions. The thirteen landscape vignettes and the gatefold do not move.** This is a completion of your own doctrine, not a new idea, and it is gated on one piece of real work: the portraits must be re-rendered or upscaled to ~2× before promotion, or you ship 208 ppi plates. If that regeneration is unwelcome, do nothing — the current build is coherent and period-correct as it stands, and "leave it" is a respectable answer.

The assemble.py changes it would take (described, not implemented):

1. **A `full` format flag in PLACEMENTS** (or infer from aspect via the art file) routing the five portraits through a new `\fullplate` command instead of `\bookplate`: essentially the gatefold's skeleton minus the width games — `\clearpage`, `\thispagestyle{empty}`, `\vspace*{\fill}`, centered `\includegraphics` sized to the measure, `\vspace*{\fill}`, `\clearpage`. Emit as a `{=latex}` fence like the gatefold (the pandoc raw-TeX heuristic issue is already documented at line 183). EPUB variant unchanged — inline images are correct there.
2. **Amend the pinning doctrine in the docstring and planning/assembly.md**: "never ahead of the anchor; behind it by at most one page break" for full plates. If the short-page-before-the-leaf under `\raggedbottom` proves ugly in practice, the fallback is a `[p]` figure float for these five only, which lets following text backfill the anchor page — a deliberate, bounded exception to the no-float rule, to be inspected in the built PDF before acceptance.
3. **Add a resolution assertion** next to the existing anchor checks: fail the build if any full-plate image falls below ~300 ppi at its laid size (`fail()` already exists; PIL is reachable via the imagegen venv that `knockout_plates()` already calls). This makes the 2× regeneration a gate the build enforces, in the same spirit as BARE_UNITS.

`plate_folios()`, the two-pass List of Illustrations, and the pre-commit rebuild hook all survive unmodified; expect downstream folios to shift by a handful of pages and the list to re-measure itself.
