Ten numbered prose excerpts from a novella manuscript. Order shuffled; headers, dates, and section titles removed.

--- Excerpt 1 ---

The visions had begun to come.

At first they were small things—shadows that moved at the edge of my sight, sounds that came from nowhere, a sense of being watched. I told myself it was the country working on my senses.

Then I started seeing the men.

--- Excerpt 2 ---

In Mexico I was made into a weapon. That is the truth of it, however I might wish to say it differently. They took the boy I was and they filed him down and they put an edge on him and they pointed him at other men. I was good at it. That is the other truth, the one I wish I could deny.

I killed seventeen men with certainty and perhaps more. Some of them I saw clearly, their faces visible in the instant before I fired. Some of them I did not see at all—just shapes in the smoke, falling when I pulled the trigger. After a time I stopped thinking about it. The weapon does not question its use.

--- Excerpt 3 ---

I read until the light failed completely, and then I built a fire at the hollow's mouth and read by the flames. I read through the night, turning page after page, watching a man's life unfold in careful, formal handwriting.

His name was Josiah Morrow. He was from Virginia. He had served in the Mexican War, at Monterrey as I had, under officers whose names I recognized. He had come back from the war broken in a way I understood.

He had found the white buffalo.

--- Excerpt 4 ---

Christmas Day. I had almost forgotten.

I am sheltering in a hollow in the bluffs, out of the wind. The snow is four feet deep on level ground, deeper in the drifts. The herd has stopped moving, clustered together in a broad valley where the snow has been beaten down by their hooves. They stand with their great heads lowered, their breath steaming, waiting for the cold to break.

I can see her from here. She stands near the center of the herd, surrounded by other animals, protected by their mass. Even in the depths of winter, she glows.

--- Excerpt 5 ---

I remembered things I had not thought about in years. The face of a man I had killed in the fighting for the Bishop's Palace—young, younger than I was, his eyes wide with surprise as my bayonet went into his chest. The sound a woman made when she found her husband's body in the street, a sound that was not quite screaming and not quite sobbing and not quite anything I had words for. The smell of the hospital tents, where men lay in rows and died by the dozens and the surgeons worked until they could not stand.

--- Excerpt 6 ---

I have found a place to shelter—a hollow in the bluffs where the wind cannot reach, where I can build small fires and survive the worst of the cold. It is not far from where the herd has gathered. I can watch them from here, can see her white form moving among the brown, can keep the vigil I have kept and will keep for however long it takes.

--- Excerpt 7 ---

I bought whiskey. Three bottles, wrapped in cloth so they would not clink. This was the supply I cared about most. I knew what waited for me if the supply ran out. I had seen it happen to other men, the shaking and the fever and the visions. I had come close to it myself, once, when a winter storm trapped me in a cabin for two weeks with nothing to drink. I did not intend to come close to it again.

--- Excerpt 8 ---

The light is growing stronger now. I can see the valley below, and the herd gathered there, and her white form standing apart from the others. She is facing east, toward the rising sun. She is waiting.

I will go to her now.

I will walk down from this ridge and cross the meadow and approach her as I have approached her a thousand times before. But this time I will not stop, and she will do what her nature demands.

--- Excerpt 9 ---

I turned back. Three hours wasted, and the morning gone with them. I rode down out of that dead valley thinking about how easy it would be to die out here. A wrong turn, a missed landmark, a night without fire, and in the morning nothing but a shape under the snow that no one would find until spring.

--- Excerpt 10 ---

I was promoted twice. Once after Monterrey, once after Buena Vista. They made me a corporal, and then they made me a sergeant, and both times they told me it was because I had shown courage under fire. But it was not courage. I could not have said what it was, then.

It was a kind of emptiness. A place inside me where the fear should have been, where the hesitation should have been, that was simply not there. When other men froze, I moved. When other men ran, I stood.

I do not know when this emptiness came to me, whether I was born with it or it was made in me. It does not matter where it came from. It was there, and the army found a use for it.
