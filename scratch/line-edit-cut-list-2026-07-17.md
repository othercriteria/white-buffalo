CUT LIST — WHITE BUFFALO (drafts 00–20, read in full)

General finding first: the journal strand's slack is not evenly distributed. Entries I–VI carry nearly all of it — the watcher/waiter/she-grows loop is stated in full roughly nine times before the settlers appear, and almost every entry closes on a groomed cadence line or a promise of arrival ("she will be ready," "that moment is coming," "I can wait"). Entries VII–IX tighten dramatically because they finally have material. The tracker's chapters are tighter throughout; their slack is mostly duplication (mission restated three times, turn-back deliberated three times, the cabin-winter withdrawal story told four times).

**1. [T1]** — 12-journal-entry-vi.md, *September the Eighth* — entire entry.
TEXT: "A year and a half. / It was spring of eighteen fifty-six when I first saw her ... She has changed in that time. So have I."
DIAGNOSIS: Pure recap. Every fact — first sighting (JE I), the purpose, the winter (JE IV) — is owned by the reader; "She has changed. So have I" is the loop's null statement. The date header alone does the calendar work, and the Sept 15 entry does the she-has-changed work in specifics.
LOSES: A soft on-ramp into the entry cluster after a chapter break. Nothing else.

**2. [T1]** — 16-journal-entry-viii.md, *January the Fourth* — final two paragraphs.
TEXT: "To whoever reads these words: I do not ask for understanding ... the only right thing I have ever done. / The white buffalo is waiting for me. I will not make her wait much longer."
DIAGNOSIS: Verbatim pre-spend of JE IX's climax: JE IX ends "This is the right thing. The only right thing I have ever done" and opens its own "To whoever finds this journal" address. Two terminal addresses one entry apart, sharing the book's key phrase, dull the second one. End the entry at "then this is the end of my account."
LOSES: Nothing. JE IX does both gestures better, and gains force by doing them once.

**3. [T1]** — 05-chapter-three.md, tavern-night section, before Council Bluffs.
TEXT: "I thought about what I was riding toward. The Nebraska Territory was wilder than Iowa ... And somewhere in that emptiness, George and Catherine Hardin had tried to make a home, and something had happened to them. / I would find out what. That was my job."
DIAGNOSIS: Near-verbatim duplicate of Chapter Two's closer ("Somewhere in that emptiness, George and Catherine Hardin had built their homestead, and I would find out what had happened to them"), plus a danger catalog already owned via Hennessey and the tavern talk. Third statement of the mission in two chapters.
LOSES: Nothing but the echo.

**4. [T2]** — 12-journal-entry-vi.md, *November the Nineteenth*.
TEXT: "She is the most beautiful thing I have ever seen. ... I am ready. I have been ready for a long time. But she is not ready yet. And so I wait."
DIAGNOSIS: Third beauty declaration (JE III Oct 23, JE V May 18), fourth-plus sacred declaration, and the ready/wait loop at its most naked — every sentence except one is restatement. The one new thing is biographical grain.
REWRITE: Keep the opening scene sentences ("She stood apart from the herd today ... the cold crept into my bones.") then cut straight to: "I saw a woman's face by candlelight once, in a time before the war. It does not compare." End there.
LOSES: The advance statement "I will not run. I will not fight" — but JE VIII Oct 26 restates the death posture in full, where it belongs.

**5. [T2]** — 10-journal-entry-v.md, *August the Twenty-Third* — entire entry, compressed.
TEXT: "I am lighter now than I have ever been ... Another winter, and she will be grown enough."
DIAGNOSIS: Beat-for-beat re-tread of JE II July 3 ("The things I carried with me—memories, desires, the weight of what I was—are falling away like dead skin"), plus "What is important is her. The waiting, the watching, the end I have chosen" (pure loop) and the fifth-or-so "almost ready" promise.
REWRITE: "I used to think about Mexico every day. Now I go days without thinking of it, and when it comes it is distant, like a story I heard once and half-forgot. The man who killed seventeen men is still here, somewhere inside me. But he is quieter now. Smaller."
LOSES: The explicit refined-or-starved question — already asked, better, in JE II ("Whether I am being refined or only starved, I cannot tell").

**6. [T2]** — 11-chapter-six.md, "The whiskey was running low" section.
TEXT: "I knew what would happen when it ran out. I had seen it before ... The shaking would start first ... Then the sweating ... the visions. / The visions were the worst part. I had heard men describe them—snakes crawling on the walls, insects covering the floor ... I did not want to go through that again."
DIAGNOSIS: A symptom forecast that pre-narrates exactly what Chapters Six through Nine then dramatize in sequence, re-treads Chapter Two's cabin-winter setup ("the shaking and the fever and the visions. I had come close to it myself, once"), and pre-spends Chapter Eight's snakes-and-insects imagery, which is doing real contrast work there ("In the cabin winter it had been the other things...").
REWRITE: "I knew what would happen when it ran out. I had been through it once, the cabin winter, and I did not intend to go through it again. But the territory was empty..."
LOSES: Named dread. The unnamed kind, arriving symptom by symptom, is stronger.

CLOSING NOTE

Estimated removal for the full T1+T2 program: roughly 1,450–1,650 words (about 5% of a ~32,000-word manuscript), of which about 950–1,050 come out of the journal strand — which is where the authors suspected it lived, and they were right. The T3 adjustments would net perhaps another 250. The book that remains is the same book with its pulse easier to feel: the journals stop promising the death and get on with earning it.

If only three: (1) item 4 — the November 19 entry compressed to the candlelight comparison; it converts the strand's most redundant entry into its most human moment. (2) Item 6 — the Chapter Six withdrawal forecast; the delirium chapters are the tracker strand's best writing and this paragraph hands the reader a program for them. (3) Item 2 — the duplicated "only right thing I have ever done"; the book's climactic sentence must occur exactly once, and right now it occurs twice.
